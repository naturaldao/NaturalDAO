# 9. Conclusion

Now, let us look back at the two major questions in Marx's exploration mentioned in the "Preface."

Why did humanity develop capitalism, a social form of extreme inequality? And what, in the end, is the ultimate force driving the ceaseless expansion of alienation and reification?

Unfortunately, the answer is already present even in Marx's critical thought!

For example, when discussing the violence of the modern tax system, Marx said:
"Thus were the agricultural people, first forcibly expropriated from the soil, driven from their homes, turned into vagabonds, and then whipped, branded, tortured by laws grotesquely terrible, into the discipline necessary for the wage system."

*Source: Karl Marx, Capital, vol. 1, ch. 28 (marxists.org transcription).*

When discussing how state violence systematically shapes the proletariat, Marx said:
"The destructive influence that it exercises on the condition of the wage labourer concerns us less, however, here, than the forcible expropriation, resulting from it, of peasants, artisans, and in a word, all elements of the lower middle class."

*Source: Karl Marx, Capital, vol. 1, ch. 31 (marxists.org transcription).*

"Violence" is the answer to its two major questions—and we all know that, fundamentally, human behavior is a product of human wisdom. Then, when we continue through deeper discussion to discover that the production line of violence, that is, the wisdom, emotional experience, and behavior related to violence, can be named "Hate 2.0," and the working mechanism of this production line can be named "Proof of Hate," everything becomes clear: it is precisely the human governance mechanism of Proof of Hate that has driven human society and every individual to a dead end. Then, by reflecting on the governance mechanism that is as distinct from it as black and white, Proof of Love is constructed—that is, we not only understand more deeply the two opposing governance mechanisms of the individual and human society, but also find the solution needed for the renewal of human civilization.

When Marx and Engels turned to historical materialism, they sublated Feuerbach's sensuous and passive "abstract love" together with his doctrine of the "species-being," attempting to interpret the evolution of human society solely through the paradigm of class struggle and productive forces. Yet what they resolutely discarded was an old idealist conception that, though lacking a practical dimension, possessed profound synthesizing power—and precisely because this rejection was too thoroughgoing, they let slip the following penetrating insight: love is by no means a single sensuous dimension running parallel to reason, but a dynamic Mind-heart-action trinity that fuses emotional experience and external action (practice) with wisdom as its governing core. And it was precisely this occlusion of the "species-being" that ultimately caused them to miss the vast possibility of setting out from "I love, therefore I am"—the deepest existential force of the human being—to explore the peaceful transition of society and the reconstruction of new relations.

From this we can see that PoL, Proof of Love, is a fundamental clarification of humanity's logic of survival and of the paradigms of individual and civilizational governance determined by it. On the basis of inheriting the Marxist dialectical tradition and deepening its vision of human liberation, it retrieves and deepens Feuerbach's fundamental insight about the "species-being" of the human being—an insight that, though expressed loosely, is extremely inspiring.

Yes, PoL, Proof of Love, inherits Marx's profound critique of capitalism—the diagnosis of "private property" and "alienated labor" remains precise; it inherits Marx's longing for the "public"—humanity must be liberated from the shackles of private ownership of the means of production, and even human reproduction and child-rearing themselves must be transformed into public affairs through technological means, so that women can be liberated from childbirth, child-rearing, and domestic labor. By returning to the species-being of the human being, that is, through the evolution of wisdom and the alignment of ethics, humanity can completely transcend bloody conflict and achieve the elevation of civilization.
