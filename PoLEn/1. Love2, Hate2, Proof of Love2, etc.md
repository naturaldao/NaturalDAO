# 1. Love2, Hate2, Proof of Love2, Proof of Hate2, PoL2 Civilization

If we regard mutual benefit as love and selfishness as hate, then in the long ages of resource scarcity (whether from natural limits or human-made outcomes) and the absence of just punishment, each person, in order to maximize their own interests, will naturally choose situationally between the "hawk strategy" (attack/selfishness, hate) and the "dove strategy" (yielding/mutual benefit, love). To maximize their own interests, most people will give priority to "hate" (the hawk strategy), keeping "love" (the dove strategy) as a fallback. This is indeed one of the most commonly used game strategies by individuals, organizations, and states, and each agent will also flip repeatedly between hawk and dove according to circumstances. Of course, the hawk-dove game is not the only strategy—for example, in situations such as arms races, a prisoner's dilemma arises in which "individual rational choice leads to collective irrationality," but the latter usually still leaves a way out, whereas the "hawk versus hawk" in the former may lead to mutual destruction.

Today, with all the wisdom of humanity, we have built a powerful technology centered on large language models. The direction in which large language models are applied can be steered toward love (serving the overall well-being of humanity), or it can be steered toward hate in loyalty to its private owner (serving the predatory purposes of a few). If the latter is chosen, then the most catastrophic outcome is not the prisoner's-dilemma-style "both lose but both survive," but the hawk-versus-hawk "mutual destruction." The current large language model rivalry, dominated by China and the United States, because it lacks fair global governance, is beginning to show signs of sliding toward this risk.

Thus we see the optimal solution to the game once material abundance has been achieved: promote love and restrain hate. Therefore, our exploration of the renewal of human civilization must necessarily be initiated by redefining core concepts such as "love" and "hate"!

## 1.2 Hate2

Love and hate are precisely a mutually entangled pair of opposites. By contrast with our description of Love 2.0, we can also give the **definition of Hate 2.0 (Hate2, Hate2)**:

> Hate is the survival wisdom, negative emotional experience, and violently destructive behavioral mode that arises in individuals or groups when facing danger, conflict, resource scarcity, or cognitive imbalance. It is often manifested through hostility, exclusion, separation, revenge, pollution, distortion, and even killing, and it not only undermines an individual's inner balance and healthy development but also damages interpersonal relations and obstructs trust and collaboration across society as a whole. Fundamentally, hate originates from the survival needs (including reproduction) of the human individual and the private strategies that arise therefrom.
> 
> Although hate sometimes evolves from survival needs, or from anger, fear, indifference, disgust, or sadistic pleasure in the face of injustice, danger, or disaster, its violently destructive forms of expression and its persistent motive are often deeply rooted in moral alienation or cognitive distortion—they are often systematically produced by unjust social structures and discourses of power, and are manifested as the narrow self-centeredness of individuals or groups (selfishness), the oversimplified attribution of complex problems (the cognitive defect of reducing the world to a friend-foe opposition), or both.
> 
> For domineering governance mechanisms, hate is an extremely efficient tool of divide-and-rule. For the oppressed, hate is the painful expression of their subjectivity, a resistance to injustice. This low-level social governance mechanism is both the pressure-release valve of life between energy and emotion and the fog between words, deeds, and relationships; it plunges individuals or groups into anxiety, fear, confusion, and even hostility, leading to the deterioration of relations and the escalation of conflict, and accelerating the tearing, disorder, and decay of society. Hatred is both an inner emotional experience and a process of putting that emotion into violent thought and action through individual action or the agitation of collective organization. The origin of hatred is based on two survival instincts: predatory hate for acquiring resources (such as killing animals for meat), and self-protective hate for responding to threats (such as protecting oneself when attacked or believed to be attacked). A note on terminology is needed here: we have fused the dual semantics that the word "文明" (wénmíng, "civilization") already carries. In Chinese, "文明" (wénmíng) is at once factual and evaluative: on the one hand it denotes the material achievements and technological progress accumulated in human history, namely the sense of the English word "civilization"; on the other hand it denotes ethical standards of conduct such as "礼貌" (lǐmào, "courtesy") and "非暴力" (fēibàolì, "non-violence"), namely the sense of the English word "civility." These two words share the Latin root cīvīlis (meaning "of the public, of the political"), which reveals that civilization itself is a composite concept fusing history, technology, and ethics. Our discussion of Civilization 2.0 encompasses both: social governance consensus and good behavior, and the shared driving force of both points to love.

But the facts are rather disheartening. Human history has developed to this day and is still mired in universal mutual oppression, alienation, and enslavement among people; human society to date remains a state in which civilization and barbarism coexist, and humanity is still on the road toward true "civilization."
