\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Our verdict for \pol{} is explicit and, on current evidence, negative for every function that decides (\cref{sec:verdict}).
Hosted \jev{} and the typed models tested beside it, and on precaution the low-cost generative evaluators tested beside them (not shown to escape these failures, most of which were never compared), should not adjudicate disputes under \art{10}, verify ethical alignment automatically under \art{6}(3), quantify persons under \clause{5.6}, or make a block of a person's speech final, because thresholds do not transfer, decisions can be steered by the content they audit and can follow option names rather than definitions, an explicit ``I don't know'' option is rarely chosen when correct and ``none'' inconsistently, and the peer evaluators to which such a model would escalate repeated almost all of its confident errors; for per-person quantification the verdict also rests on precaution, since no study measures error by speaker group.
For the functions that detect, the safety valve of \clause{5.4.1}, the truncation of \clause{4.3.3.1} and the anomaly detection of \art{7} on text (not on numeric or structured signals without a trained model), hosted \jev{} is usable only as a recorded, three-valued, locally calibrated \emph{detector} inside the design of \cref{sec:design}, in which automatic action requires agreement between independently built detectors, any restriction of a person is brief and reversible, every chain that could block ends with a person, confident automatic decisions are audited, and every decision is recorded; the public-decision assessment of \clause{5.6} is usable in the same sense, as an audited aggregate measurement with its measured error carried into every conclusion.
Even that detector verdict is an extrapolation from English benchmarks of binary hate speech: no study measures the specification's three-valued construct in Chinese, the only Chinese emotion result found every system weak, and hate against merely offensive language was separated weakly by every system tested.
The certainty of every finding behind these verdicts is low or very low, four findings are contested, and an update search to 8~October reversed none of them; a single well-designed study on the specification's own construct could change any of them.

Typed decision models nonetheless arrived with the interface that automated governance safeguards need: a typed verdict with a probability, for every input and output, at a cost that makes universal screening affordable.
What transfers from this assessment to \pol{} regardless of model is a set of practices tied to that interface: three-valued questions with sufficiency asked separately, neutral identifiers bound to definitions and tested by renaming, a record of every decision, local calibration with temporal re-validation, agreement gating between differently built detectors, human-ended chains with blind audit of confident decisions, probabilities kept from untrusted callers, unneeded sensitive fields kept out of the input and untrusted queries restricted and logged, independent flags asked in separate calls, scoring of events rather than persons, and, before any of these can be measured rather than extrapolated, a construct benchmark in Chinese.

For \pol{}, several of the framework's own commitments are consistent with what the evidence recommends: the state of absence, the warning that love and hate are not moral labels, and the distinction between persons and behaviours.
Other provisions are sharpened by the evidence into choices the text has yet to make: whether to draw an explicit line between understanding emotion in language and inferring persons' emotional states; how to ask three-valued questions of models that say ``insufficient'' unreliably; how to reconcile transparency with closed models; whether non-intervention should yield, for consequential decisions, to a human who can suspend them, close to what data-protection law requires where it applies; and whether per-person assessment can be squared with equal connection.
Making these choices explicit, and testing the resulting safeguard on the specification's own construct, is the work that follows.
