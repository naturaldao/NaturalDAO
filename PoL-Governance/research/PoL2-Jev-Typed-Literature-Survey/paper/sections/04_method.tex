\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

To test the seven requirements against the evidence, we assessed every study of typed decision models that bears on at least one of them.
The assessment is therefore narrower than a general review of typed decision models, because a study enters it only if it bears on G1--G7.
We followed PRISMA~2020~\cite{page2021prisma} and the Cochrane rapid-review guidance~\cite{garritty2021cochrane} where they apply.
No protocol was registered in advance. The last search run followed a written protocol, and \oa{B} states where the analysis departs from it and gives the queries, the selection counts and the codebook.

\paragraph{Who did what.}
The author defined the research question, the requirements, the codebook and the decision rules, and is responsible for every judgement in the paper.
AI agents (Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic) ran the searches, screened records, extracted data from full texts with locators, coded and appraised the studies, served as the blind second coder, wrote the figure scripts, drafted text that the author revised, and checked the numbers in the text against their sources in separate review passes. The AI-use statement gives the full disclosure.
The author did not re-code or re-check individual codes by hand, and no human screened or coded in duplicate.

\paragraph{Eligibility and search.}
We included studies whose object, experimental component or direct comparator is a typed decision model, defined as a model that returns a probability distribution over options declared by the caller, or a probability for a declared statement, without generating free text.
The first public version had to be dated from 15~September (the release of \jev{}) to 8~October 2026, and any language and design was eligible.
Grey literature was included when it reports original measurements of a typed model; it was not searched systematically and is marked \gmark{}, and Zenodo records are marked \zmark{}.
We searched arXiv with 18 queries on 1, 4 and 8~October, and Zenodo with 11 queries on 1 and 8~October.
We also searched two community trackers~\cite{xiao2026allaboutjev,rafe2026awesome} before 1~October and on 8~October, and Hugging Face Papers, OpenReview, SSRN and the web before 1~October. The search was completed on 8~October 2026.

\paragraph{Corpus.}
After screening by title, abstract and full text, the corpus contains \nArxivAll{} arXiv papers, \nZenodoAll{} Zenodo records and one paper available only on alphaXiv (\nStudies{} studies in all), together with \nGreyAll{} grey-literature sources.
We coded the \nCodedAll{} sources that report results bearing on at least one requirement, namely \nCodedStudies{} of the \nStudies{} studies and the \nGreyAll{} grey sources.
The other studies are engineering papers outside the requirements, tools without an evaluation, non-empirical items, or \nBackgroundTier{} background-tier arXiv records that are counted in the corpus but neither coded nor appraised.

\paragraph{Coding.}
We coded each pair of a study and a requirement that it informs for \emph{detector} use, with a codebook that treats supporting and negative results symmetrically.
A pair is coded \emph{supports} (S) if the primary result shows the typed model performing the function at least as well as its comparators, or meeting the study's own criterion, without an extra condition that the study had to add.
It is coded \emph{negative} (N) if the model fails the function or does clearly worse than its comparators, and \emph{qualifies} (Q) if the result is mixed or depends on an added condition such as a locally fitted threshold, recalibration, a restriction of language or domain, or human review.
Conditions that make up a requirement's test, such as adversarial content for G2 or name and order perturbations for G3, are not added conditions (decision rule~(iv) in \oa{B}).
For each pair we also recorded how the typed model compared with any generative evaluator on that requirement, and we did not record label-probability readouts of the same backbone as comparators.
Because studies differ in tasks, metrics and designs, we did not pool effects; the codes count studies and are not effect sizes~\cite{campbell2020swim}.

\paragraph{Second coding.}
A second AI coder, blind to the first coding, coded \nKappaNAll{} pairs: 30 comparable pairs from a random sample of 32 from the records of the runs of 1 and 4~October, and all 118 pairs from the records added by the run of 8~October.
The two coders agreed on the code in \nKappaAgreeAll{} pairs (Cohen's $\kappa = \nKappaAll{}$, bootstrap 95\% CI \nKappaAllLo{}--\nKappaAllHi{}) and on the comparator in \nCompKappaAgree{} ($\kappa = \nCompKappa{}$). Disagreements were resolved by re-reading the source or by a third AI pass.
This agreement has two limits.
First, decision rule~(iv) was missing from the codebook file used by most of these coders, so $\kappa$ measures an earlier state of the codebook rather than its final form (\oa{B}).
Second, a content review on 10~October recoded eight codes that contradicted the coders' own rules, and \oa{B} lists them.

\paragraph{Appraisal.}
We appraised each coded study, and each non-empirical or context item, on independence, design and the reporting of sample sizes and uncertainty.
A study is of \emph{stronger design} if it was preregistered or held-out, reported sample sizes with intervals or tests, \emph{and} evaluated a system that its authors did not build.
It is of \emph{weaker design} if it was a single run, a case study or non-empirical, or reported neither sample sizes nor uncertainty, and \emph{moderate} otherwise.
Of the \nAppraisedAll{} appraised sources, \nStrongerAll{} are of stronger design, \nModerateAll{} moderate and \nWeakerAll{} weaker. None is peer reviewed.

\paragraph{Certainty ratings.}
For each key finding we rated the certainty of the evidence in the spirit of GRADE~\cite{guyatt2008grade}, which starts non-randomised evidence at \emph{low}.
A finding is \emph{very low} if it rests on a single study, on studies none of which is of stronger design, or, when it concerns hosted \jev{} or typed models generally, on open-model evidence only.
It is \emph{low} if at least two studies agree in direction and at least one is of stronger design, and \emph{moderate} if at least three independent stronger-design studies agree and at least one is preregistered or independently replicated.
In these ratings, though not in the descriptive tallies of \cref{sec:evidence}, studies sharing an author count as one.
Findings about hosted \jev{} or typed models generally are rated on hosted-model evidence alone, with open-model studies as corroboration.
A study that points the other way marks a finding \emph{contested}, and lowers its rating by one level if it ranks at least as high as the strongest supporting study.
A script, \nolinkurl{scripts/rate_certainty.py}, computes the ratings from every coded study's assignment to each finding and checks them against \cref{tab:keyfindings}, and \oa{C} lists, for each finding, the studies counted for and against it.
