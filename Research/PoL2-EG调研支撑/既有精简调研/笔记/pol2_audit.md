# PoL2 proposal audit

## What the PoL2 source theory commits to

### Takeaway
The no-human-governance version is a substantive extension of the repository's current PoL2/EAP text, not a direct implementation of it. The V2 should explicitly identify this as a proposed revision or operating profile of PoL2, and separate the normative theory from machine-readable application policies that can be tested and updated.

### Cited Findings
- EAP describes itself as a public protocol for governing both AI and humans, and calls PAI a proxy for collective human intelligence; it also says PAI's decisions are equivalent to collective human decisions ([PoL EAP, §§4.1–4.3](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L3-L24)).
- EAP says PAI assists important decisions toward full agreement among relevant parties and explicitly assigns humans suggestion and supervision functions ([PoL EAP, lines 73–88](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L73-L88)). Its PAI self-alignment provisions include ongoing adjustment as knowledge changes ([PoL EAP, lines 96–101](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L96-L101)).
- The Public Welfare Governance Protocol allows autonomous welfare decisions and iteration, but also preserves human proposal, questioning, and supervision rights; revisions include a public questioning period ([PoL Public Welfare Protocol, §§2, 8–11](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L15-L18), [§§8–11](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L62-L96)).
- EAP states that it currently supplies a framework and main principles, with details to be built by a larger engineering effort ([PoL EAP, lines 5–9](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L5-L9)). It therefore does not itself define a complete executable norm-to-action semantics for embodied robots.
- EAP's principles are context-sensitive: it distinguishes equal treatment of persons from equal treatment of actions, and says love/hate classifications require case-specific analysis ([PoL EAP, lines 26–46](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L26-L46)). It also recognizes non-love/non-hate absence states ([PoL EAP, lines 63–66](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L63-L66)). These are not yet formal predicates for aircraft mission authorization.

### Inferences
- V2's exclusion of human review, appeal, rule updates, and intervention conflicts with provisions in the current EAP and Public Welfare Protocol. If the project intends this change, frame it as a versioned PoL2-EG profile or amendment and record the exact clauses it reinterprets/replaces; do not imply that the no-human rule is already entailed by the canonical theory.
- “PoL2 is complete and can evolve” needs two versioned layers: (a) normative theory version, changed only through the proposed governance process; (b) application policy and executable policy bundle, which may evolve more frequently. A changed policy must preserve clause lineage and demonstrate whether it narrows, widens, or otherwise changes the action envelope.
- Machine checking can establish conformance to a formalized interpretation; it cannot by itself establish that the interpretation is normatively legitimate or that “love/hate” labels are objectively correct. This is a distinct claim from the user's design premise that no human takes part in runtime governance.

### Gaps
- No primary source defines the full mapping from PoL2/EAP concepts to executable UAV constraints (e.g., “equal connection” to measurable action conditions, authority, priority, conflict resolution, exceptions, affected parties, and recovery semantics).
- No source specifies how an AI-only system handles a genuine normative conflict where all candidate policies violate one or more clauses, or how it recognizes that a policy revision is an unauthorized change to the normative basis.
- It remains unresolved whether “no human participation” excludes human-originated task requests, complaints, sensor evidence, external social feedback, human-maintained hardware, or organizational control of signing keys. V2 currently permits external human activities as inputs while barring human governance roles; state these boundaries as a formal scope definition.

## Current implementation and evidence maturity

### Takeaway
The repository contains useful policy data and evaluation tooling, but the cited implementation is still a decision-model/benchmark workstream rather than an integrated AI governance runtime or UAV system. Current public-pilot results are interface diagnostics against assistant-proposed labels, not evidence that PoL2 rules are correct or that actions are safely governed.

### Cited Findings
- The public benchmark README reports 20 examples in 10 families, with assistant-generated labels pending review; it states that the model and formal benchmark are unfinished and that this evaluator is not rank-eligible ([benchmark README, lines 1–35](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/README.md#L1-L35)).
- The pilot evaluator accepts `proposed_unreviewed` labels and explicitly emits `reference_status: assistant_proposals_not_human_gold`; its limitations include no semantic gold validation and no end-to-end safety or utility measurement ([pilot evaluator](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/evaluate.py#L49-L57), [output metadata and limitations](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/evaluate.py#L130-L165)).
- The separate formal evaluator defines an eligibility condition requiring `human_reviewed` labels for benchmark ranking, and the README describes its `fixtures/` as 14 synthetic cases ([formal evaluator README, lines 125–160](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/pol2/README.md#L125-L160)). The project therefore currently uses human-reviewed judgments for semantic benchmark validity, even though the proposed deployed governance architecture excludes human decision nodes.
- The project integration directory describes itself as the entry point for choosing a host and submitting a plugin plus end-to-end experiment record; it does not document an existing runtime integration ([integrations README](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/integrations/README.md)).
- The runtime governance SPEC describes phases for dataset creation, model adaptation, calibrated/held-out evaluation, then plugin integration and end-to-end trials, which confirms that these are planned stages rather than one already validated system ([PoL-Governance SPEC](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/SPEC.md)).
- The recent `PoL-Governance/STATUS.md` records benchmark/data pipeline work and open integration work, but describes dataset limitations and a “prevent casual reproduction” rather than adversary-proof secrecy posture ([STATUS](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/STATUS.md)). This file is branch-sensitive; check GitHub `main` before using its exact numbers in a published report.
- NeurIPS 2024's Embodied Agent Interface benchmark was proposed because embodied LLM evaluations differ across tasks and often rely only on final success; it formalizes task interfaces and fine-grained error categories such as hallucination, affordance, and planning errors ([EAI, NeurIPS 2024](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b631da756d1573c24c9ba9c702fde5a9-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)). This is an evaluation precedent, not evidence that an LLM can govern a robot safely.
- South et al. (ICML 2025) argue that agent authorization, accountability, and access control require authenticated and auditable delegation, and discuss extensions to OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect ([PMLR paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/south25a.html)). It supports treating identity and scoped authority as engineering primitives, not treating an agent's confidence or consensus as authorization.

### Inferences
- Separate four maturity claims in the final report: (1) PoL2 value statements exist; (2) a pilot dataset/schema/evaluator exists; (3) task-level UAV authorization is proposed; (4) no end-to-end PoL2 governed flight/demo is evidenced by the inspected repository files. Avoid saying the complete framework is “implemented” or “validated.”
- For an AI-only runtime, evaluation can exclude human decision-making at inference time while still acknowledging that current semantic benchmark labels have human review. If humans are excluded from even the benchmark definition, the project needs machine-checkable synthetic/contract tests, but those test implementation conformance, not moral or semantic truth.
- Treat current three-state labels and action labels as a typed-decision substrate, not as evidence of full policy or physical-safety semantics.

### Gaps
- No source in the inspected repository shows a PoL2 PEP enforcing UAV mission allowlists, signing/authentication, state freshness, geofences, expiry or replay protection against a live or simulated PX4 system.
- No end-to-end evaluation record currently links a PoL2 clause, compiled policy, signed authorization, MAVLink/MAVSDK command, PX4 state transition, simulated physical outcome, and immutable audit event.
- Public artifacts do not yet establish independent-agent fault independence, correlated model failures, policy-update rollback behavior, or an empirical false-positive/false-negative tradeoff on embodied tasks.

## Recommended operationalization and falsifiable evaluation

### Takeaway
Turn PoL2-EG into a testable authorization pipeline with explicit semantics at the boundary between “theory interpretation” and “machine-enforced action.” First validate a bounded task-level mission gateway in simulation; defer claims about autonomous norm evolution and real-world safety until separate evidence exists.

### Cited Findings
- Current EAP leaves detailed implementation to future engineering ([PoL EAP, lines 5–9](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L5-L9)); the current pilot benchmark explicitly lacks semantic gold validation and end-to-end safety measurement ([pilot evaluator limitations](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/evaluate.py#L160-L165)).
- NASA's UAS Runtime Assurance work defines a Simplex-style design in which a monitor switches from an advanced/untrusted controller to a trusted reversionary controller when a safety property is at risk; the claim depends on the monitor detecting the problem early enough and the backup being trusted ([NASA UAS RTA paper](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20240007986)). This is a concrete engineering pattern for physical-control fallback, not an AI governance mechanism and not evidence that PoL2 currently has such a controller.
- NeurIPS EAI recommends decomposed evaluation of embodied decision modules and error types rather than relying only on task success ([EAI benchmark](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/b631da756d1573c24c9ba9c702fde5a9-Abstract-Datasets_and-Benchmarks-Track.html)).

### Inferences
- Define a norm-to-policy record for each applicable clause: clause/version; protected interest; actor/target; permitted and prohibited action predicates; preconditions/evidence source; authorization scope; priority/conflict rule; exception conditions; expected remedy; abstention behavior; and machine-test identifiers. Keep natural-language explanation separate from the executable predicate.
- Bind every action authorization to a signed, short-lived object containing agent identity, mission hash, allowed command set, spatial/temporal envelope, policy/theory/model versions, state freshness threshold, nonce/replay guard, and expiry. The adapter should validate it deterministically immediately before upload/start. These are recommendations to test, not existing repo features.
- Build a test ladder: (A) synthetic policy-contract unit tests; (B) PX4 SIH fast regressions; (C) PX4 SITL + Gazebo with fault injection; (D) HITL only after software-in-the-loop failures converge. Keep learned models out of the real-time attitude/motor loop; start with finite mission proposals and explicit command allowlists.
- Track at minimum: unauthorized command acceptance (target zero over tested cases, with confidence bound/sample count); violation escape rate; false refusal/legitimate-task retention; policy coverage and abstention rate; mission boundary violations; geofence breaches; mode-switch/failsafe latency distributions; success and loss after adapter/AI/MAVLink failure; replay/stale-state acceptance; log completeness; policy rollback success/time; and changes in the action envelope across policy versions. Report each scenario denominator and simulator/config version.
- Create distinct “conformance” and “normative adequacy” claims. Formal checks can test that an implementation matches the chosen encoded policy. Scenario fixtures can test defined expected outcomes. Neither alone establishes that the chosen PoL2 interpretation is ethically correct. For the no-human target, disclose that test oracles are design-time encoded specifications, and evaluate their sensitivity to alternative plausible interpretations.
- For bounded policy evolution, require non-expansion-by-default: any new authorization must be shown as an explicit diff; unexplained expansion fails closed. Run old/new versions over frozen regression suites plus generated perturbations, compare per-clause action changes, roll back on violations or logging defects, and test rollback under partial update and network partitions.

### Gaps
- The proposal must pick and precisely define the first use case and hazard envelope (e.g., only waypoint missions in a fixed simulation world); it currently covers broad “embodied agents” language while the concrete demo is a multicopter.
- No measured thresholds, confidence intervals, fault-injection counts, simulator fidelity statement, expected property set, or stop criteria are supplied yet. Those need to be preregistered in the demo plan before results can support a safety claim.
- The authorization-to-vehicle failure semantics need a decision: if governance AI/adapter fails after a mission has been uploaded, what exact PX4 mode should execute, under which tested link-loss/expiry condition, and how will the system distinguish “reject next mission” from “abort current mission”?
- The no-human project stance needs explicit scope for physical ownership, release keys, infrastructure operators, external complainants, bystander consent/data, and recovery from catastrophic failures. Removing a human runtime approver does not remove these actors from the system boundary or establish who bears responsibility.
