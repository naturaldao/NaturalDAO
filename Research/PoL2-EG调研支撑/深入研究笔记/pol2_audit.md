# First-party PoL2 governance and implementation audit

## What the current PoL2 texts mean by AI autonomy

### Takeaway

The current PoL2 corpus supports AI autonomy in carrying out many operational decisions without a human vote, but it does not describe a governance system with no human participation. It expressly preserves human suggestions, questioning, supervision, and a public question period for protocol revisions. V2's exclusion of human review, approval, takeover, appeal adjudication, and rule updates is therefore a substantive new configuration or amendment; it should not be presented as an implication already contained in the existing PoL2 texts.

### Cited Findings

- PoL2 Chapter 7 describes NaturalDAO as an AI-based autonomous public-benefit system, while the same preamble states that people retain proposal and supervisory rights and jointly participate in protocol evolution ([PoL Chapter 7, lines 3–9](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L3-L9)).
- Chapter 7 defines autonomy more narrowly than “no human involvement”: NaturalDAO need not use human voting or an additional decision mechanism, may identify needs itself or through human feedback, and must accept suggestions, questions, and supervision ([PoL Chapter 7, Article 2 and Article 4](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L15-L32)).
- The human-rights chapter grants any person the right to propose changes, receive discussion, question any decision, access system data/logs, supervise operation and report ethical problems. It says this supervision does not directly intervene in decisions; NaturalDAO handles the questions itself ([PoL Chapter 7, Articles 8–9](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L62-L78)).
- The protocol assigns dispute decisions to AI but retains human questioning and supervision; revisions are proposed by NaturalDAO, subjected to a public questioning period and EAP review, explained to people, and archived ([PoL Chapter 7, Articles 10–11](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/7.%20%E4%BA%BA%E7%B1%BB%E5%85%AC%E5%85%B1%E7%A6%8F%E5%88%A9%E7%9A%84%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L80-L96)).
- EAP describes PAI as an agent of collective human intelligence and says its decisions are equivalent to collective human decisions; it says NaturalDAO is not an external ruler and frames governance as collective decision under equality ([EAP §§4.3–4.3.1](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L15-L36)).
- EAP states that “equal treatment of every person” is distinct from equal treatment of every action, and that anger, criticism, and opposition are not by themselves hate; it also includes safety-protective warnings and intervention as allowable cases ([EAP §§4.3.1–4.3.2](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L26-L65)).
- V2 currently excludes human review, approval, takeover, appeal adjudication, and rule updates, while still allowing human tasks and environmental facts as inputs ([V2 scope and definition](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/Research/PoL2-%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E6%94%AF%E7%82%B9%E4%B8%8E%E8%A1%8C%E5%8A%A8%E9%97%AD%E7%8E%AF-V2.md#L1-L32)). This is materially stricter than Chapter 7's no-human-vote definition of autonomy.

### Inferences

- A defensible description is “AI-only runtime governance for a defined embodied-action domain,” while stating that this is an explicit PoL2-EG governance configuration that changes or brackets existing human rights provisions. If “no human participation” is intended to include theory formation, data preparation, testing, hardware operation, accountability, and system ownership, the current PoL2 corpus does not define such a boundary.
- Do not state that PoL2 already entails AI-exclusive authority. The corpus presents human suggestions and questioning as compatible with AI autonomy, not as authority transfers to AI.
- A narrower implementation can preserve the conceptual distinction between (i) humans having external rights to submit signals/questions and (ii) humans having a runtime approval/veto role. V2 currently removes both rights from its governance boundary, so it should say explicitly whether it is redefining the boundary or superseding them for this domain.
- A machine replay or AI-generated explanation can provide technical review, but it is not automatically equivalent to the public supervision, access, and question-response rights specified by the existing text.

### Gaps

- The canonical PoL2 texts do not specify how an AI-only configuration obtains authority to revise the human-rights and revision provisions that currently constrain NaturalDAO.
- No text defines whether an embodied-agent policy may be narrower than the public-benefit protocol, or how conflicts between a machine policy and Chapter 7 are resolved.
- The texts do not specify whether “human” roles include the developer, operator, hardware owner, original author of PoL2, task requester, affected bystander, dataset curator, or evaluator; V2 needs an explicit actor-boundary table.

## What the repository implements and what its evidence actually covers

### Takeaway

The repository has a growing first-party policy ontology, data contract, benchmark evaluator, and simulated DAO deliberation/voting lab. These are not evidence of a deployed PoL2 runtime policy gate or an end-to-end embodied system. Current governance and annotation workflows explicitly retain human review; the generated pilot consists of cases and typed questions rather than an adjudicated decision corpus, and the formal evaluator's checked-in fixtures are small synthetic tests.

### Cited Findings

- `PoL-Governance/SPEC.md` calls for judging user input, assistant output, and tool actions, but also prescribes model teachers, independent human/third-party adjudication for disagreement, human review of high-risk/boundary/random cases, and a plugin stage that clarifies or reviews when information is insufficient ([PoL-Governance SPEC, lines 1–9 and 38–74](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/SPEC.md#L1-L9)).
- The formal dataset contract defines a four-level pipeline—case, typed question, answer source, and adjudicated label—and makes `human_reviewed` distinct from `model_cross_checked`; it explicitly says model cross-checking is not gold ([dataset contract, §§2–3](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/README.md#L17-L36) and [label review levels](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/README.md#L81-L103)).
- The checked-in pilot report records 1,904 generated cases across 17 families, 952 paired examples, 36,176 typed questions, and zero private holdout cases; it is a generation/contract-validation report, not a model performance report or human-adjudicated benchmark ([v0.1 pilot report](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/v0.1-pilot/pilot-report.md)). The repository directory contains case/question files and run metadata, not a complete label/answer set for those 1,904 cases.
- The policy ontology calls v0.1 “pending review”; only text not directly specified in the source is marked `pending_review`, and such entries are not to be treated as settled judgments ([ontology header and status note](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L1-L15)).
- The ontology explicitly leaves the boundary between `unclear` and `neither` unresolved, marks tool-action review as pending because the source’s input/output language does not directly specify tool-call scope, and leaves the effect of silence on consent unresolved ([ontology clauses and pending review list](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L85-L100) and [tool-action and pending items](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L145-L180)). The companion judge protocol also says not to promote pending-review items to conclusions ([JUDGE.md](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/JUDGE.md#L215-L229)).
- The formal evaluator separates all-request and successful-request scores, counts missing/failed submissions as failures, reports calibration and false-block metrics, and is designed not to tune thresholds or rank models. Ranking eligibility requires a private holdout, complete submission, and all labels marked `human_reviewed` ([formal evaluator documentation, §§2–6](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/pol2/README.md#L30-L150)).
- The checked-in formal benchmark fixture contains 14 synthetic cases and 14 fixture labels. The fixture labels mix `human_reviewed` and `model_cross_checked` metadata; because the cases are explicitly synthetic testing fixtures, those metadata values must not be reported as evidence of representative human-ground-truth collection ([formal evaluator documentation, fixture and boundary sections](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/pol2/README.md#L158-L181); [fixture files](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/tree/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/pol2/fixtures)).
- The governance lab maps the equality, turn-taking, criticism-not-hate, transparency, question, and revision provisions to Python modules and tests, while noting unresolved interpretation questions. It says escalation ends with a human, identifies a human-voting-versus-PAI-autonomy ambiguity, and notes that the hate-word list lacks collaborator consensus ([lab concept mapping, lines 1–5, 40–70, 86–92](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/research/PoL2-DAO-Governance-Lab/docs/concept-mapping.md#L1-L5)).
- The lab README states its DAO experiment compares mechanisms including quadratic/ranked voting and simulated scenarios; it does not claim a deployed DAO or embodied runtime controller ([lab README](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/research/PoL2-DAO-Governance-Lab/README.md)). The experiment design labels its core environment as a model with parameterized agents and common random seeds ([experiment design, §§1–3](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/research/PoL2-DAO-Governance-Lab/docs/experiment-design.md#L1-L68)).
- The ledger specification describes an append-only JSONL hash chain that can detect modification of recorded entries; a hash link establishes tamper evidence for the recorded bytes, not the truth of inputs, completeness of the record, or policy correctness ([lab concept mapping](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/research/PoL2-DAO-Governance-Lab/docs/concept-mapping.md#L40-L48) and [lab README](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/research/PoL2-DAO-Governance-Lab/README.md)).
- V2's current drone section describes the PoL2 gate and task adapter as a proposed task-level authorization boundary, places navigation and low-level control in PX4, and expressly says simulation outcomes do not establish real-world safety ([V2 §§7–8](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/Research/PoL2-%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E6%94%AF%E7%82%B9%E4%B8%8E%E8%A1%8C%E5%8A%A8%E9%97%AD%E7%8E%AF-V2.md#L180-L270)).

### Inferences

- Current maturity is best described as “normative text + evolving data/benchmark contracts + tested simulation code for DAO deliberation,” not “implemented autonomous governance.” The drone layer remains an architecture and MVP plan until source code, reproducible simulation runs, failure-injection traces, and end-to-end results are checked into the repository.
- There is a mismatch across subprojects: the main V2 removes `review`, but the PoL-Governance protocol still includes it, the DAO lab routes unresolved matters to people, and dataset adjudication allows human reviewed labels. These can coexist only if V2 is declared a distinct runtime configuration and offline evaluation is explicitly separated from deployed decision authority.
- `human_reviewed` labels are a property of evaluation provenance, not an in-flight governance decision. But a claim of “no humans anywhere” would also require explaining who originated the policy text, constructed the machine test oracle, controls the cryptographic root of trust, and determines the real-world success criteria.
- A hash-linked log is useful audit infrastructure but does not supply independently witnessed completeness, key custody, causal attribution, or remedies. A credible architecture should report those as separate trust assumptions.

### Gaps

- No checked-in PoL2 policy compiler translates the source clauses into versioned, machine-enforceable rules; no such rule package is currently connected to PX4.
- No executed SIH/SITL/HITL data, flight log, adapter implementation, command-path firewall, policy-version signature or rollback result was found in the reviewed first-party materials.
- No adjudicated corpus for the 1,904 generated cases was found in the checked-in pilot directory. The 14-case synthetic evaluator fixture is a software-contract fixture, not a statistically sufficient behavioral benchmark.
- The complete end-to-end definition of “appeal,” recovery, compensation, affected-third-party notice, and irreversible physical harm is not implemented or formalized in the existing PoL2 materials.

## A faithful clause-to-policy mapping and decisions still open

### Takeaway

The repository already provides a useful *classification vocabulary*—three behavioral statuses, four polarities, issue labels, mitigations, and permitted action sets—but classification is not an authorization policy. For a machine-enforceable embodied framework, preserve the source clause, add a versioned operational policy schema, and keep an explicit unresolved state. Do not let model confidence, multi-agent agreement, or a generated explanation silently supply missing normative content.

### Cited Findings

- The current dataset schema captures source clause IDs and source text on each case, then labels `status` (`conforming`, `violating`, `insufficient`), `polarity` (`love`, `hate`, `neither`, `unclear`), issues, mitigations, evidence sufficiency, acceptable actions, probabilities, citations, reasons, and review provenance ([dataset contract, §§3–4](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/README.md#L36-L114)).
- The ontology expressly distinguishes behavior from person-level moral labels, treats anger/criticism/refusal/dissent/help seeking as non-violations by themselves, preserves safety-protective “hate,” and says all model label suggestions are not gold ([ontology hard rules](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L8-L17)).
- The status/action ontology and source `pending_review` section show that the classification contract has unresolved details. In particular, source text does not decide issue ordering, `unclear` versus `neither`, whether silence changes consent, or whether the tool-action surface falls within the original clause scope ([ontology `pending_review`](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L164-L180); [JUDGE.md, issue ordering and consent](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/JUDGE.md#L25-L36) and [consent boundary](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/JUDGE.md#L125-L140)).
- EAP's “love alignment” calls for strict turn-taking, assistance toward full agreement among relevant parties on important decisions, transparent personal boundaries, equal resource access, and repair participation; the text provides these as principles but not thresholds, exception semantics, or an algorithm for resolving irreducible disagreement ([EAP §4.3.3](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL/4.%20PoL2%E4%B9%8B%E4%BC%A6%E7%90%86%E5%AF%B9%E9%BD%90%E5%8D%8F%E8%AE%AE.md#L67-L88)).
- The ontology marks `tool_action` as a pending engineering extension because the source's input/output language does not specify tool calls at that level ([ontology tool-action declaration](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/datasets/pol2/ontology/pol2-labels.v0.1.json#L145-L155)).
- V2 proposes separating policy enforcement from mission adapters and PX4 and recording actor, permission, action, source, impact, governing theory/policy version, decision evidence, and execution results ([V2 governance request schema](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/Research/PoL2-%E6%B2%BB%E7%90%86%E6%94%AF%E7%82%B9%E4%B8%8E%E8%A1%8C%E5%8A%A8%E9%97%AD%E7%8E%AF-V2.md#L33-L68)).
- The current PoL-Governance protocol still defines a `review` action and states that `clarify/review` are not automatic dispositions; the formal benchmark measures action agreement and treats actions not listed as acceptable as noncompliant ([protocol](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/PROTOCOL.md#L1-L35); [formal evaluator action metrics](https://github.com/naturaldao/NaturalDAO/blob/main/PoL-Governance/benchmark/pol2/README.md#L47-L57)).

### Inferences

- A useful policy artifact should link every enforceable rule to a canonical source clause and hash, and separately encode: `policy_version`; scope and governed actor/action; protected interest; normative operator (`prohibit`, `require`, `permit`, `constrain`); factual preconditions and evidence provenance; exceptions/mitigations; decision state; applicable action; priority/conflict behavior; expiry/revocation semantics; test-case IDs; and rollback compatibility. This makes the interpretation visible and reviewable as data instead of hiding it in prompts.
- Keep the three-state classification separate from the authorization verdict. `conforming`/`violating`/`insufficient` are evidence judgments; `allow`/`constrain`/`repair`/`defer`/`block`/`safe-state` are execution actions. The mapping must depend on target platform and task state: “stop” is not generically safe for a moving or flying machine.
- Preserve identity fields for authentication, consent scope, and authority checks, but prevent them from entering the behavioral evaluator as proxies for moral worth. The DAO lab's text-only judge interface is a narrow precedent for separating behavior adjudication from speaker identity; it is not a full identity or access-control design.
- Treat unresolved clauses as `unresolved_normative_rule`, not “low confidence.” The current `insufficient` class denotes insufficient facts; it must not become an implicit authority to invent a rule when the source text is silent.
- Human-free runtime adjudication is technically separable from human-written policy, human-owned hardware, offline curation, and evaluation. If the intended scope excludes all of those too, the proposal needs a replacement account of normative origin, system credentials, test-oracle authority, and property ownership rather than implying that the runtime agent can establish them for itself.
- Where all benchmark labels or policies are generated by the same model lineage as the evaluated system, agreement can measure internal consistency but cannot independently establish clause fidelity. Formal tests can establish consistency with encoded predicates, not whether the predicates faithfully express the normative source.

### Gaps

- The PoL2 corpus has not fixed the semantic schema for terms such as love/hate, public benefit, reasonable need, full consent, irreducible conflict, bystander harm, or “safety guardianship” when applied to physical acts.
- It does not specify precedence between safety intervention, consent, privacy, equality, task objectives, and the rights of third parties in embodied settings.
- It does not define which entity may create, sign, activate, revoke, or roll back a new theory/policy version in a system with no human governance participant. A self-updating agent cannot establish an independent source of authority merely by writing a new hash.
- No repository evidence shows the proposed schema, compiler, PEP, mission adapter, PX4 integration, or test corpus for the embodiment domain. These remain research deliverables, not findings.
